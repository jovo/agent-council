# Orientation tuning in mouse V1 after monocular deprivation

## Question

Does four days of monocular deprivation in adult mice change the orientation selectivity of layer 2/3 neurons in binocular V1?

## Method

We imaged layer 2/3 neurons with two-photon calcium imaging in 8 adult mice, before and after four days of deprivation of the contralateral eye. Gratings drifted in 12 directions, 10 trials each. Trials in which running speed exceeded 2 cm/s were excluded, since locomotion changes visual gain. We computed an orientation selectivity index (OSI) per neuron and averaged it within each mouse, so the mouse is the unit of analysis.

## Results

Mean OSI fell from 0.41 to 0.33 after deprivation (paired difference across mice, −0.08, 95% CI −0.12 to −0.04). Responses to the open eye did not change detectably (−0.01, 95% CI −0.05 to 0.03), so these data cannot tell whether the open eye's selectivity shifted.

## Interpretation

Deprivation is associated with lower orientation selectivity for the deprived eye in adult binocular V1. Because every mouse was deprived, the design has no undeprived control for time or repeated imaging.
